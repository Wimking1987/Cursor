## 1. Executive Summary

Classic‑car ("Oldtimer") owners face a chronic problem: for cars 25+ years old,
many parts — especially **interior, trim, and cosmetic components** — are simply
no longer manufactured. Original parts are either unavailable, prohibitively
expensive on the used market, or crumbling with age (typical for plastics,
switches, vents, clips, knobs, and trim).

We solve this with **additive manufacturing (3D printing) plus 3D scanning and
CAD**. We reverse‑engineer a part from a sample or reference, reproduce it to
fit and finish, and deliver either a **one‑off replacement** or a **small
production series**. Because we already own professional‑grade equipment (a
**Formlabs Form 3L** resin printer, a **Bambu Lab (X1‑class) FDM printer**, and a
**3D scanner**), our marginal cost to serve each order is low and our quality is
high.

We monetise three ways:

1. **Reproduction spare parts** — a growing catalog of ready‑to‑order parts for
   popular classic models (highest margin, recurring).
2. **Print‑on‑demand service** — customers send a model (or a physical sample);
   we print, finish, and ship (high volume, steady).
3. **Prototype development & reverse engineering** — paid engineering/CAD/scan
   work for restorers, small manufacturers, and makers (highest value per hour).

**Objective:** reach **monthly operating profit by month 7–9** and **recoup the
initial investment (cumulative break‑even) by roughly month 12–14**, on a lean
cost base and organic + targeted marketing. See
[`02-financial-model.md`](02-financial-model.md).

## 2. Company Overview

### 2.1 The problem

- Classic and youngtimer vehicles are a large, growing, emotionally‑invested
  market, but the parts supply chain shrinks every year.
- **Interior and trim parts** are the worst affected: air‑vent slats, dashboard
  switches, window‑crank handles, ashtray parts, console trim, clips, emblems,
  knobs, grilles, and mounting brackets. These are low‑volume, so no OEM or
  aftermarket supplier reproduces them profitably by injection moulding.
- Owners currently rely on scarce used parts, expensive specialists, or "make
  do." There is real willingness to pay to restore a car correctly.

### 2.2 The solution

A **digital‑to‑physical** workshop:

`Sample or reference → 3D scan / CAD → optimise for printing → print (FDM/SLA) → finish (sand, prime, paint, dye) → QA / fit check → ship`

We keep the **CAD/scan file** for every part we make. Over time this file
library becomes the company's most valuable asset: each new file can be sold
repeatedly at near‑zero marginal cost.

### 2.3 Vision, mission, values

- **Vision:** Become the go‑to European source for hard‑to‑find classic‑car
  interior and trim parts produced on demand.
- **Mission:** Keep classic vehicles on the road and looking right, one
  reproduced part at a time.
- **Values:** Authentic fit and finish, honest communication about what is/is not
  reproducible, respect for intellectual‑property and safety limits.

## 3. Products & Services

### 3.1 Reproduction spare parts (catalog)

Ready‑to‑order reproductions of specific, verified parts. We start with **1–2
vehicle platforms we can source reference parts for**, prove fit, photograph the
result, and list them. Each proven part is repeatable and becomes catalog
inventory‑on‑demand (we print only when ordered — zero dead stock).

- **Best first targets:** non‑safety‑critical interior/trim plastics (vents,
  knobs, switch caps, clips, brackets, escutcheons, emblems, grilles, covers).
- **Materials:** ABS‑like / ASA / PETG / Nylon (FDM) for functional and UV‑exposed
  parts; tough/durable resins (SLA) for fine‑detail, smooth‑surface parts.

### 3.2 Print‑on‑demand (POD) service

Customers upload an STL/STEP file **or** send a physical sample; we advise on
material, print, finish, and ship. Serves classic‑car people *and* the wider
maker/small‑business market (jigs, fixtures, enclosures, architectural models,
props, replacement plastics for appliances, etc.). This diversifies revenue
beyond automotive.

### 3.3 Prototype development & reverse engineering

Paid engineering service, billed hourly or per project:

- 3D scanning of an object and conversion to a clean, editable CAD model.
- Design/redesign of a part (improve strength, fix a known weak point, adapt
  fitment).
- Iterative prototyping for inventors and small manufacturers before they commit
  to tooling.

This is the **highest‑margin activity per hour** and builds reputation and
referrals.

### 3.4 Finishing & value‑add (differentiator)

Raw prints rarely satisfy a restorer. We offer: support removal, sanding,
priming, painting to color code, texturing/graining to mimic OEM surfaces,
dyeing resin parts, vapor smoothing where suitable, and assembly of
multi‑part components. Finish quality is our moat versus hobbyist competitors.

## 5. Market Analysis

### 5.1 Market context (DACH / Europe)

- Germany alone has a **large and steadily growing classic‑vehicle population**
  (vehicles registered with the historic "H" plate number in the high hundreds
  of thousands, with the broader 20–30‑year‑old "youngtimer" pool far larger).
- Owners are typically **older, higher‑income, and emotionally committed** —
  they spend on originality and correctness, not just the cheapest fix.
- The **restoration and parts‑reproduction niche is fragmented**: many tiny
  specialists per marque, few offering modern digital reproduction.
- Broader tailwind: **distributed / on‑demand manufacturing** is a fast‑growing
  segment; customers increasingly accept "printed" as a legitimate production
  method.

### 5.2 Target customers

1. **Private classic‑car owners & enthusiasts** — need a specific missing/broken
   part; value correctness and are willing to wait and pay.
2. **Restoration workshops & specialists** — recurring B2B; they need parts they
   cannot source and prefer a reliable partner over one‑off eBay hunts.
3. **Marque clubs & online communities** — concentrated demand for the same
   known‑problematic parts; excellent for catalog seeding and word‑of‑mouth.
4. **Makers / small manufacturers / inventors** — the POD + prototyping side;
   diversifies away from automotive seasonality.

### 5.3 Demand signals to validate early

- Which specific parts do clubs/forums repeatedly ask for? (Build the catalog
  around *proven repeat demand*, not guesses.)
- What are people currently paying (used‑part prices, specialist quotes)? That
  sets your price ceiling.

### 5.4 Competition

| Competitor type | Strength | Our advantage |
|-----------------|----------|---------------|
| Used‑parts sellers (eBay, scrapyards) | Cheap, genuine | We deliver *new*, available *on demand*, in *repeatable* quality |
| Marque specialists / small reproducers | Deep model knowledge | We're digital, faster to add new parts, cross‑marque |
| Generic 3D‑print shops | Capacity | We have automotive‑fit expertise, finishing, and a scan workflow |
| Hobbyists printing at home | Free/cheap | Professional finish, fitment guarantee, resin capability, reliability |

**Positioning:** *"The right part, made to fit and finish like the original —
even when nobody else can supply it."*

## 7. Operations Plan

### 7.1 Standard workflow

1. **Intake** — customer describes the part (photos/measurements) or ships a
   sample; we quote.
2. **Digitise** — 3D scan the sample or model from references.
3. **CAD** — clean up mesh → parametric model; fix known weak points if desired.
4. **Print prep** — orient, support, choose material, slice.
5. **Print** — FDM and/or SLA.
6. **Post‑process** — wash/cure (resin), support removal, sand, prime, paint/dye,
   texture, assemble.
7. **QA / fit check** — dimensional check; test fit against sample where possible.
8. **Ship** — protective packaging; tracked shipping; archive the file + job sheet.

### 7.2 Capacity (planning assumption)

- Printers run largely **unattended and overnight**, so machine hours are cheap;
  the true bottleneck is **founder labor** (scanning, CAD, finishing, admin).
- Budget realistic **billable/productive hours per week** and protect them; this
  is the real constraint on the ramp in [`02-financial-model.md`](02-financial-model.md).

### 7.3 Suppliers & consumables

- Filament (ASA/PETG/PA‑CF), SLA resin, IPA, sandpaper, primer/paint, packaging.
- Keep 2 vetted suppliers per key consumable to avoid stockouts.

### 7.4 Quality management

- Written **fit‑check protocol** and a photo log per part.
- Version control on CAD files (a part revision = a new file version).
- Clear **"reproducible / not reproducible / not safety‑critical only"** policy.

## 8. Marketing & Sales Strategy

**Principle:** go where classic‑car owners already gather; let proof‑of‑work do
the selling.

### 8.1 Channels

- **Marque‑specific forums & Facebook groups** — post before/after reproductions;
  answer "does anyone have part X?" threads with a solution.
- **Instagram / YouTube / TikTok** — short "scan → print → fit" transformation
  videos are highly shareable and build trust fast.
- **Own web shop** — a clean catalog + an "upload your file / send us a sample"
  quote form. (Shopify/WooCommerce, or Wix if preferred.)
- **Marketplaces** — eBay/Etsy for reach; POD platforms (e.g. Craftcloud, Treatstock)
  to capture generic print demand early.
- **Club partnerships** — offer clubs a "problem‑parts" program; they bring
  concentrated demand, you give them a member discount.
- **B2B outreach** — restoration workshops in your region; be their outsourced
  "parts we can't find" department.

### 8.2 Trust builders

- Public **before/after gallery**, dimensional accuracy claims, and a
  satisfaction/fit guarantee within reason.
- Transparent lead times and honest "yes we can / no we can't" answers.

### 8.3 Sales funnel

Inbound request → quick quote (target < 24 h) → deposit for custom work → produce →
deliver → **ask for a review + photo** → add proven part to catalog → market it.

## 9. Business Model & Pricing

See full numbers in [`02-financial-model.md`](02-financial-model.md). Summary:

- **Catalog parts:** fixed price = (material + machine + finishing time) × margin.
  Target **60–75% gross margin**; anchor price to used‑part scarcity, not just cost.
- **POD:** priced per job (material + machine time markup + finishing). Minimum
  order value to protect against tiny unprofitable jobs.
- **Prototyping / scanning / CAD:** **hourly rate €60–90/h** (or fixed project
  quote), reflecting skilled engineering value.
- **Rush surcharge** for expedited jobs.

Revenue mix goal by month 12: a healthy blend of recurring catalog + POD (volume,
predictable) and prototyping (high margin, lumpy).

## 10. Legal, IP & Regulatory (read carefully)

> This is not legal advice — confirm with a German lawyer / tax advisor. But the
> following materially affects what you may safely sell.

- **Intellectual property / design rights:** Reproducing parts that carry a
  manufacturer's **logo, trademark, or a protected registered design** can
  infringe rights. **Safest zone:** functional, generic, or clearly out‑of‑protection
  parts, and parts you sell as **"reproduction, not OEM."** Avoid copying trademarked
  logos/emblems for sale. When in doubt, get permission or redesign.
- **Safety‑critical parts:** Do **not** start with brakes, steering, suspension,
  fuel/electrical‑safety, or structural parts. Interior/trim/cosmetic parts carry
  far lower liability. Document this policy.
- **Type approval / TÜV (§ 21 StVZO, ABE/Einzelabnahme):** cosmetic interior parts
  generally don't affect roadworthiness; anything that could, needs proper
  approval. Stay in the low‑risk lane initially.
- **Product liability & disclaimers:** clear T&Cs, "reproduction part / fitment is
  buyer's responsibility for correctness," and **liability insurance** (Betriebs‑
  /Produkthaftpflicht).
- **Business setup:** register the Gewerbe; decide Kleinunternehmerregelung (§ 19
  UStG) vs. regular VAT; get a tax advisor. Consider UG/GmbH once liability
  exposure grows.
