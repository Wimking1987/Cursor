# Financial Model & Path to Profit (Year 1)

> All figures in **EUR (€)** and are **planning assumptions**. They are built to
> be *credible and conservative*, not optimistic. Swap in your real quotes and
> first‑order data as you get them. Two things drive everything: **(a) how fast
> orders ramp** and **(b) how many productive founder hours you protect.**

## 4. Pricing model (how each € of revenue is built)

| Service | Basis | Target |
|---------|-------|--------|
| **Catalog reproduction part** | (material + machine time + finishing time) × margin, anchored to used‑part scarcity price | 60–75% gross margin |
| **Print‑on‑demand** | material + machine‑time markup + finishing; **min. order €25** | ~65% gross margin |
| **Prototyping / reverse‑engineering / scan‑to‑CAD** | hourly €60–90 (or fixed quote) | ~90% gross margin (mostly time) |
| **Finishing add‑on** | per‑part or per‑hour | high margin, strong differentiator |
| **Rush surcharge** | +30–50% | protects the schedule |

**Worked example — a reproduced interior vent:**
material €3 + machine time €4 + finishing 0.5 h ≈ €18 cost → **sell €49–69**
(anchored to the €80–150 people pay for a scarce used one). Gross profit
≈ €31–51 per unit, repeatable from the same file forever.

## 6. Founder pay & "real" profitability

A business only truly "makes a profit" once it covers the founder's living wage
**and** repays what was put in. Assume a target **founder draw of €2,500/month**.

- **Operating profit (EBIT) turns positive in month 3.**
- The business can **fund a €2,500/month founder wage from ~month 6–7** (when EBIT
  ≥ €2,500) — this is the practical *"I'm making a living from it"* milestone.
- From month 7 onward, EBIT − €2,500 wage is still **positive and growing**, so
  the company is profitable *even after* paying the founder.

## 7. Break‑even analysis (two honest views)

**View A — you already own the equipment (your actual situation):**
You only need to recoup the **€6,000 fresh cash**. Cumulative EBIT crosses
**+€6,000 in month 7** → *fresh‑cash break‑even ≈ month 7*, and comfortably
within the year.

**View B — count the full €24,000 invested capital (incl. equipment), for a bank/investor:**
Cumulative EBIT crosses **+€24,000 between month 10 and 11** → *full‑capital
payback ≈ month 11* **if profits are reinvested (no founder draw).** If the
founder instead takes the €2,500/month wage from month 6, full‑capital payback
shifts to roughly **month 14–16** — still fast for a manufacturing business.

**Monthly operating break‑even point:** fixed cost €1,100 ÷ 75% margin ≈
**€1,470 of revenue per month** covers all fixed costs. You clear that in
**month 3**.

## 8. Sensitivity — what moves the result

| Lever | If it improves | If it worsens |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| **Order volume / marketing** | Ramp pulls forward 1–2 months | Everything slips; guard cash |
| **Prototyping hours billed** | Highest‑margin €; each extra 5 h/week ≈ +€1,500/month | Under‑utilised founder time is the #1 profit killer |
| **Gross margin (finishing, failed prints)** | +5 pts margin ≈ +€3,000 over year 1 | Reprints/returns erode margin fast — QA matters |
| **Fixed costs** | Home‑based start (‑€400 rent) pulls break‑even earlier | Renting too early adds fixed drag |
| **Catalog compounding** | Each proven file = passive repeat sales | Slow to build if you chase only one‑offs |

**Fastest path to profit:** (1) start home‑based to keep fixed costs low,
(2) bill prototyping/scan hours aggressively from week 1, (3) convert every
custom job into a *catalog* part so revenue compounds, (4) protect founder
productive hours ruthlessly.

## 9. Assumptions to validate in the first 60 days

1. **Real demand:** list the top 15 parts your target clubs/forums repeatedly ask
   for. Do they convert to orders?
2. **Real pricing:** what do used parts / specialists actually charge? (sets your ceiling)
3. **Real cost per part:** log material + machine + finishing time on your first
   10 jobs and update the 75% margin assumption.
4. **Real capacity:** how many productive hours do you *actually* protect per week?

Update this model monthly with actuals — a business plan is a living document,
not a one‑time exercise.
